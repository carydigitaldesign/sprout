# M5: IPC

Date: 2026-11-02. Driver: Claude (Fable 5.1). Navigator: Andre.

## What exists now

- **Channels** (`obj/channel.rs`): a pair of endpoints. A message is up to
  4 KiB of bytes plus up to 8 handles. Handles travel as `Entry` values
  (object plus rights): `channel_send` requires the transfer right on each,
  removes them from the sender's table all at once, and `channel_recv`
  inserts them into the receiver's. A buffer too small leaves the message
  queued; a dropped endpoint marks its peer closed, and receiving from a
  closed, empty endpoint is an error. `recv` takes a timeout.
- **Ports** (`obj/port.rs`): attach channel endpoints with a key; `wait`
  returns the first attachment with a signal asserted (`READABLE`,
  `PEER_CLOSED`), or times out. Level-triggered: endpoints keep a list of
  observing ports and wake them on every change; `wait` re-polls.
- **Timed blocking** (`sched`): a blocked thread may carry a deadline; the
  timer tick readies it when due. `WaitQueue::wait_while_until` returns
  whether the condition cleared or the deadline won.
- **Spawning from userspace**: `process_spawn(image_vmo, bootstrap, name)`
  copies the ELF out of a VMO, moves the bootstrap handle into the new
  process as `HANDLE_BOOTSTRAP`, and returns a process handle with the read
  right, which `process_wait` needs. `init` receives the `shell` image as
  its bootstrap handle from the kernel, since nobody spawns `init`.
- **Shared memory**: `vmo_map` maps a whole VMO into the caller at the next
  free address above `USER_MMAP_BASE` (with a guard page), writable if the
  handle has the write right; `vmo_unmap` removes it. Two processes mapping
  one VMO share its pages. `handle_duplicate` makes a second handle with a
  subset of rights, so a sender can keep its own.
- **`shell`** (`user/shell`): a command interpreter over its bootstrap
  channel: `ping`, `echo`, `read-vmo` (maps a received VMO and answers with
  its text), `describe`, `exit`.
- **`init`** now spawns `shell` and checks the whole path: ping/pong, echo,
  a port that times out when idle and wakes on a reply, a VMO written and
  mapped here, modified through the mapping, handed over with read-only
  rights and read back by the shell, the shell's exit code, and the
  peer-closed signal afterwards.
- Twenty syscalls. `describe` covers channels (pending messages, peer
  closed) and ports (attachments).

## Decisions made along the way

- **Handle numbers are reused.** After the bootstrap handle moved out, its
  slot went to the new process handle. `init`'s first check assumed the
  number was dead and closed the shell instead; the fix was to ask what the
  number refers to (`describe`), not whether it exists. Rule for userspace:
  a handle number means nothing once you have closed or transferred it.
- **Send validates everything, then moves everything.** Transfer rights are
  checked for all handles and the peer's state is checked before any handle
  leaves the table, so a failed send loses nothing.
- **User program ELFs are stripped of debug info** at build time
  (`CARGO_PROFILE_*_STRIP=debuginfo`). The kernel embeds them whole and
  `process_spawn` copies an image through the heap; with debug info `init`
  was 1 MiB and did not fit. Now about 230 KiB.
- **Kernel heap is 4 MiB** (was 1), for the same reason.
- **Ports poll, endpoints wake.** Simpler than edge-triggered packet queues
  and correct on one hart; a packet is never lost because a signal that is
  still true is reported on the next `wait`.
- **No `Thread` or `AddressSpace` objects yet.** Nothing in M5 needed to
  name them; they arrive with multi-threaded processes.

## Known limitations

- A process has one thread.
- `channel_recv` with a too-small buffer gives no hint of the needed size.
- `vmo_map` always maps the whole VMO at a kernel-chosen address; no
  offset, no fixed address, no partial unmap.
- Messages are copied twice (user to kernel, kernel to user); fine at 4 KiB.
- `process_spawn` copies the whole image out of the VMO rather than
  mapping its pages directly.
